How we’ll work

  1. Open the phase the facilitator introduces.
  2. Take a few minutes to write your own observations.
  3. Share one observation per turn when invited, starting with one you most want the group to hear. Passing is fine.
  4. After the facilitator captures it, tick “Shared in discussion.” Keep the note—do not delete it.
  5. Move to the next phase when prompted.
  6. At the end, download all your feedback and email the file to the facilitator.

Your notes stay on your screen during the session. The facilitator’s shared board is a separate discussion record. You do not need to read every note aloud. Marking a note as shared does not send it or confirm that the facilitator has saved it.

Writing tips & examples

A starting point—not a required form.

A simple format

During [activity or handoff], [what worked, was challenging, or deserves attention]. [Effect on your work, if relevant.]

One observation per note, usually one or two sentences. You don’t need to explain the cause or propose a solution. Use your own words—the format is only a guide.

Choose a colour

Examples only—not findings about this survey.

✓ Green — What went well

“During questionnaire testing, the feedback summary made it easy to see which comments had been addressed.”

! Coral — What was challenging

“During processing validation, I wasn’t always sure which file was the current version. I had to confirm before continuing my review.”

★ Yellow — Worth future attention

“The handoff between processing and certification may deserve further attention, particularly clarity about when files are ready for review.”

You don’t need a note in every category. A challenge does not need a matching improvement note. The star flags something to explore—not an approved solution, priority, or commitment.

Writing rules

  • Put one observation on each note.
  • Name the activity, handoff, or situation—not just the overall phase.
  • Describe your experience in specific, neutral language.
  • Add the effect on your work if useful.
  • Focus on the process, not individuals or assumed intentions.
  • Causes and solutions are not required.

You can contribute to any phase you experienced, including handoffs. You do not need a note for every activity.

Make it clearer

Instead of…Try…
“Communication was bad.”“During collection, I wasn’t sure where to find updates about changes to the collection period.”
“Testing was good.”“During EQ testing, the issue log made it easy to track outstanding items.”
